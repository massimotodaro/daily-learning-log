## DL-2026-09-21-01 — Bootstrap questions are business decisions; and a correction on operator sign-in

**Areas:** Security and privacy; Business and leadership

**Tags:** `bootstrap`, `authentication`, `corrections`

### What I learned

Who creates the very first administrator, when no administrator exists yet, is not a technical puzzle. The answer defines the root of trust, so it belongs to the business owner.

### What I did

The owner answered: the root of trust is his existing cloud-dashboard account, where he creates one owner identity whose id is held in a secret, never in code; only that owner creates operator accounts. Operators and the owner now sign in with email, password and an authenticator code, with manual key entry and recovery codes.

### Evidence or example

Correction: on 2026-09-08 I recorded one sign-in flow for everyone with no authenticator. That still holds for school users; it no longer holds for platform operators, who now require an authenticator.

### Why it matters

Resolving a bootstrap question by the shape of existing code can quietly decide who controls the whole system.

### Open questions

- How is the owner identity recovered if the owner loses both device and recovery codes?

### Next step

Stop and ask whenever a design needs something to exist before itself.
