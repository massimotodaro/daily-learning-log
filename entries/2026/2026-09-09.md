## DL-2026-09-09-01 — Show and record legal notices; do not ask a child to agree

**Areas:** Security and privacy; Education and domain knowledge

**Tags:** `transparency`, `legal-design`, `children`

### What I learned

Being told about how your data is used and agreeing to a contract are different things. In a school product the school is responsible for the processing; students need clear information, not a button that asks them to accept terms they cannot legally be bound by.

### What I did

I recorded the model for the first-sign-in screen: it shows the applicable documents and records that they were shown. The only acceptance in the product is the school's signed agreement.

### Evidence or example

Asking a minor to accept terms could also imply a direct relationship between the software supplier and the child, which the controller/processor arrangement is designed to avoid.

### Why it matters

Good intentions ('let's get agreement') can create legal exposure. Separating transparency from agreement keeps the product both honest and correctly structured.

### Open questions

- Should a school be able to ask for an acknowledgement of its own rules through the app, and whose record would it be?

### Next step

Keep the first-sign-in screen show-only, and test that no end-user acceptance wording appears in it.
