## DL-2026-09-03-01 — Lock a published version before anything references it

**Areas:** Software engineering; Education and domain knowledge

**Tags:** `versioning`, `immutability`, `data-modelling`

### What I learned

When one record points at another that can still change, the pointer silently changes meaning. An assignment given to students must point at a frozen version of the lesson, not at the live lesson a teacher may keep editing.

### What I did

I settled a set of architecture decisions for lessons and assignments: an assignment binds only to a lesson that is ready, and binds to that lesson's locked version rather than to the lesson itself.

### Evidence or example

The decision records separated the reusable lesson record from the version an assignment consumes, so a later edit creates a new version and never rewrites what students already received.

### Why it matters

Students, marking and evidence all depend on knowing exactly what was set. Immutable versions make that question answerable years later without archaeology.

### Open questions

- When should an old version be retired, and what happens to assignments still pointing at it?

### Next step

Treat 'reference a mutable record' as a design smell and ask which frozen version should be referenced instead.
