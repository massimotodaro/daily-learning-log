## DL-2026-09-08-01 — Ask instead of inferring when a request seems to reverse a settled decision

**Areas:** Business and leadership; Product and design

**Tags:** `requirements`, `communication`, `decision-records`

### What I learned

A request can read as if it overturns a decision made the day before. Inferring either way is risky; asking one precise question usually shows the decision stands and the request means something narrower.

### What I did

A request to let teachers 'edit criteria' looked like it would reopen how marking criteria are stored. I stopped and asked. The answer: teachers select, remove, substitute and weight pre-approved criteria, but never write criterion text.

### Evidence or example

The clarification was recorded explicitly as confirming, not changing, the earlier decisions.

### Why it matters

One question saved a day of rework and kept the content library controlled by the organisation rather than by each user.

### Open questions

- How can decision records make it easier to spot a request that conflicts with them?

### Next step

When a request seems to contradict a recent decision, quote the decision back and ask which reading is meant.
