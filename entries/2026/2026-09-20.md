## DL-2026-09-20-01 — Name an AI agent as a system, never as a person

**Areas:** AI and agent systems; Education and domain knowledge

**Tags:** `anthropomorphism`, `children`, `product-safety`

### What I learned

Product safety guidance for educational AI asks that an assistant does not give an impression of personhood: no first-person 'I', and no human name, avatar or character. A friendly human name for a chatbot is a compliance problem for a children's product, not a branding detail.

### What I did

The assistant was renamed with a neutral system name, and every user-visible string that addressed or described it was changed. The rule is enforced in the model's highest-priority instructions, in output verification and in an evaluation.

### Evidence or example

The question later extended to the product name itself, which led to a rebrand toward an institutional name.

### Why it matters

Anthropomorphism can encourage emotional dependence in young users. Naming is one of the cheapest places to prevent it.

### Open questions

- How should the output checker handle quoted text that legitimately contains 'I'?

### Next step

Check new user-facing copy for first-person or personifying language before release.
