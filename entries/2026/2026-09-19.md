## DL-2026-09-19-01 — Fewer origins, fewer permissions

**Areas:** Software engineering; Product and design

**Tags:** `permissions`, `workflow`, `simplicity`

### What I learned

If no user can ever create a task for another user, the entire 'who may assign work to whom' permission model disappears. Constraining where records come from can delete whole subsystems.

### What I did

I recorded that a work item has exactly two origins: a person's own task, or one raised by the system from an admitted event. System-raised items cannot be moved or deleted by hand; their status derives from the source.

### Evidence or example

The same review found that nothing in the platform ran on a clock, so one scheduled-job path was planned once to serve several time-based needs.

### Why it matters

Asking 'where can this come from?' is often a faster route to simplicity than asking 'who may do this?'

### Open questions

- Which other entities could be restricted to system-raised origins?

### Next step

List the origins of each new record type before designing its permissions.
