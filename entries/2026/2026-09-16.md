## DL-2026-09-16-01 — Never invent a value: keep one ledger of real settings

**Areas:** Tools and workflow; Business and leadership

**Tags:** `configuration`, `hallucination`, `process`

### What I learned

An invented example address, written into two task briefs, was followed literally. Mail sent to it was lost. Plausible made-up values are more dangerous than obvious placeholders.

### What I did

I created one ledger of every real, non-secret operational value — addresses, accounts, projects, regions, URLs and the names (never the values) of secrets — each row marked verified or unconfirmed. No task may name a value that is not in it.

### Evidence or example

The rule applies to people and AI agents equally: if a value is needed and is not in the ledger, the answer is to ask.

### Why it matters

Agents and humans both treat written instructions as fact. A single verified source stops a guess from becoming configuration.

### Open questions

- Should a check fail any task brief that names an address missing from the ledger?

### Next step

Update the ledger from every report that changes a setting, in the same sitting.

## DL-2026-09-16-02 — One alert per open safeguarding flag, not one per message

**Areas:** Education and domain knowledge; Product and design

**Tags:** `safeguarding`, `alert-fatigue`, `notifications`

### What I learned

Repeated alerts about the same concern train people to ignore alerts. For safeguarding, that habituation is the real risk.

### What I did

I recorded the rule that escalating a concern sends one email at the moment it is raised or promoted, that the email says it was promoted when it was, and that no further emails are sent while that flag stays open; the flag record is the working surface.

### Evidence or example

A new concern after a flag is closed is a new flag with its own email. The rule lives in a versioned policy, not as a constant in code.

### Why it matters

Notification design is safety design: the goal is that the one important email is read, not that many are sent.

### Open questions

- What should happen if the one email bounces?

### Next step

Treat alert volume as a safety metric, not only an operational one.
