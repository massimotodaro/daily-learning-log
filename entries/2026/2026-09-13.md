## DL-2026-09-13-01 — Simplicity is a design pass: delete options before adding defaults

**Areas:** Product and design; Software engineering

**Tags:** `simplicity`, `permissions`, `scope`

### What I learned

Earlier rulings can be changed when a simpler path appears. A small permission model that matches how schools actually work beats a flexible one that nobody configures.

### What I did

In one batch, a system of seventeen teacher rights and two bundles was replaced by one rule: a teacher teaches the classes and subjects an administrator assigns. A second, overlapping assignment mechanism was removed.

### Evidence or example

The batch was recorded with the decisions it superseded, so the history stays readable.

### Why it matters

Every option is a state to test, document and support. Removing options is often the fastest way to make a system correct.

### Open questions

- Which remaining settings are configured by nobody in practice?

### Next step

Before adding a setting, check whether one fixed rule would serve every real case.
