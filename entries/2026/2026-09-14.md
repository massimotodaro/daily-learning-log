## DL-2026-09-14-01 — Build to the stricter legal reading when it is cheap

**Areas:** Business and leadership; Security and privacy

**Tags:** `compliance`, `risk`, `decision-making`

### What I learned

When paid legal review is deferred, a useful test is: if counsel later requires the conservative answer, would changing the build cost an afternoon or a quarter? Anything that would cost a quarter is built the strict way now.

### What I did

I recorded a compliance-by-design loop: an assumptions ledger (each assumption, why, what would falsify it, cost if wrong) and a build-decisions file, reviewed together as development moves. A previously deferred deletion capability was brought back into scope because it would be expensive to add late.

### Evidence or example

The deferral of paid review was explicitly a cost decision, not a risk decision: no real student data enters the system before a signed agreement.

### Why it matters

It turns vague legal worry into a ranked list of cheap-now versus expensive-later choices.

### Open questions

- Which assumptions carry the highest cost if wrong but the lowest cost to test now?

### Next step

Keep the assumptions ledger current and reread it whenever stored data or processors change.
