## DL-2026-09-04-01 — A control nobody reads is not a control

**Areas:** Cloud and delivery; Business and leadership

**Tags:** `monitoring`, `alerting`, `operations`

### What I learned

A failure that is logged but never read is operationally the same as no detection at all. A dashboard is just another place nobody looks unless a named person reads it on a schedule and acts.

### What I did

I recorded a standing rule after finding a background worker that had failed on every run for nineteen days while writing errors to a log that nobody watched. It was found only when someone computed deployment drift by hand.

### Evidence or example

The errors were visible the whole time. The missing piece was a reader: a summary with green, amber and red that reaches a human the moment it is ready.

### Why it matters

Adding more alerts to unread places increases cost without increasing safety. Every new control now has to name who reads it and how the result reaches them.

### Open questions

- Which controls can be read by an automated summariser, and which need a human every day?

### Next step

For any new monitoring job, write down its reader and delivery channel before building it.
